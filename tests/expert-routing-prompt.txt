Explain how a mobile computer executes a mixture of experts language model. Begin with the input text and describe tokenization, embeddings, causal attention, the router, expert selection, and the weighted combination of expert outputs. Discuss why the number of tokens assigned to an expert changes which processor can execute it efficiently. A central processor can handle small tasks with low overhead, while a neural processor can use large matrix units for larger batches. Consider the cost of moving activations between processors and waiting for both branches to finish before the next layer begins.

Now consider a sequence of 256 input tokens. Each token selects several experts, but the load is not necessarily balanced across experts. Some experts may receive many tokens and others only a few. Describe an experiment that compares running all experts on the central processor, running all experts on the neural processor, and assigning experts according to their token counts. Keep the weights resident so that weight loading does not affect the comparison. Measure the complete elapsed time for one Transformer layer and then for two successive layers. Include attention, shared experts, routing, synchronization, and aggregation in the total. Explain why two layers cannot be evaluated as independent workloads when the second layer depends on the first layer's actual output.

Finally, discuss how to verify numerical correctness, why warmup is necessary, and why measurements should alternate policy order. Describe the difference between a latency reduction in this small experiment and a speedup for a full model serving realistic conversations on a phone. Consider thermal effects, CPU scheduling, memory bandwidth, and the overhead of launching small jobs on an accelerator.
