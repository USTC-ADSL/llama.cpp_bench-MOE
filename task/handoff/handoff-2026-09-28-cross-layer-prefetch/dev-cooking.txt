A community kitchen prepares hot lunches for people with different dietary requirements. Some ingredients arrive through donations, so the menu cannot be planned exactly a week in advance. The kitchen has limited refrigerator space and two ovens that heat unevenly. Volunteers rotate frequently and do not all have professional cooking experience. Propose an approach to planning meals, checking ingredient labels, and assigning tasks during the busiest hours. Explain how to keep allergen information attached to each batch of food, how to record substitutions, and what to do when a delivery arrives late. Include a practical way to estimate portions without encouraging excessive waste, and describe how staff should review problems after service.
