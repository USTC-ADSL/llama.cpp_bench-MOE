A village is reviewing the operation of its drinking water reservoir after several unusually dry summers. The reservoir receives water from a forest stream, while a separate treatment plant filters the water before it reaches homes. Farmers have proposed using reclaimed water for irrigation so that the reservoir can be reserved for essential household needs. The village engineer wants to compare the options without assuming that next year's rainfall will match the historical average. Explain how the council should collect evidence and make a practical decision. Discuss measurements of inflow, storage, leakage, daily demand, treatment capacity, and seasonal variation. Describe how uncertainty should be communicated to residents and why a reserve for emergencies matters. Include a simple monitoring plan that volunteers can help maintain, but distinguish their observations from calibrated instrument readings. Consider how changes in electricity prices might affect pumping schedules, and explain why reducing leakage may be useful even if the reservoir is expanded. Avoid inventing local rainfall statistics or claiming that any single intervention is guaranteed to solve the problem. End with a short sequence of actions the council could take during the next three months, including how to review progress if the weather changes unexpectedly.
