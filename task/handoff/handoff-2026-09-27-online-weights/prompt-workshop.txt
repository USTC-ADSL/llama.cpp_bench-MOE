A small cooperative is opening a workshop that repairs household furniture and teaches practical woodworking skills. The building has two large workbenches, a separate finishing room, storage shelves, and a narrow loading entrance. Members have different levels of experience, and the workshop will hold public classes on weekends while accepting repair jobs during the week. A local school has offered to donate old chairs and tables for students to restore.

Develop a practical operating plan for the first three months. Explain how to schedule the benches, track unfinished jobs, organize hand tools, and choose projects suitable for beginners. Include an example showing how a damaged chair moves from inspection through repair and final evaluation. Discuss how the cooperative can estimate material costs before accepting a job, deal with missing parts, and communicate delays to customers. The plan should leave time for cleaning, tool maintenance, and instruction. Finally, suggest a simple method for recording lessons learned from unusual repairs so that future members can benefit from the experience without needing elaborate software or lengthy paperwork.
