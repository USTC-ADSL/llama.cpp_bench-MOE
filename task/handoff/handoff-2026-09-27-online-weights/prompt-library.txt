A neighborhood library has received a donation of several thousand books and a small grant to improve access for its readers. Its storage room is nearly full, its catalog contains duplicate entries, and some donated books have missing pages or handwritten notes. The librarians want to preserve useful material without overwhelming the shelves. Readers include young children, students preparing for examinations, older adults, and recent immigrants who speak several languages. Propose a fair process for inspecting, cataloging, selecting, and distributing the donation. Explain how volunteers should record the condition of each item and when a trained librarian should review a difficult decision. Include a simple system for recognizing duplicate editions without assuming that the newest edition is always the best one. The plan should protect personal information found inside donated items and provide an accessible way for readers to request material that is temporarily in storage. Describe how to use the grant so that the library does not create maintenance expenses it cannot afford next year. Finally, suggest how staff can evaluate the project after six months using borrowing patterns, reader feedback, and the amount of time spent locating books. Avoid relying on expensive software or assuming that every visitor has internet access.
